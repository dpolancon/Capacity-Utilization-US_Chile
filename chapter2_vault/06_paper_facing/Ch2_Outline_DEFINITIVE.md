# Chapter 2 Definitive Outline & Paragraph Micro-Outline

## I. Foundational Metadata & Context

*   **Chapter Title:** Internally Divided Firm and Market Anarchy: Growth and Distribution in Center and Periphery
*   **Relationship to Chapter 1:** Direct continuation. Chapter 1 demonstrated that the US aggregate output–capital relation fails to cointegrate in isolation, requiring the rate of exploitation ($e_t$) in a trivariate VECM to establish stability and yielding a long-run transformation elasticity $\hat{\theta} < 1.0$ (structural overaccumulation). Chapter 2 provides the micro-meso foundations for this distributional mediation and extends it comparatively to Chile's peripheral formation.
*   **Writing Calibration:** Styled in the authoritative, active-voice register of Chapter 1. Complies with the `/umass-applied-econometrics` (BLUF by second paragraph, non-parametric visualization first, endogeneity/distribution as endogenous drivers, specification-space mapping, descriptive macro grounding, institutional critique) and `/humanize-writing` (no AI transitions, active voice $\ge 70\%$, passive voice $< 30\%$, hedging density $< 5\%$, sentence length variety) principles.

    *   *Focus:* State that capacity utilization is not a technical constant but a socially mediated outcome of capitalist reproduction. Introduce the empirical approach (FM-OLS/VECM interaction specifications) and state the core comparative result: a US overaccumulation regime ($\hat{\theta} < 1.0$) vs. a Chilean balance-of-payments constrained regime, where capacity formation is mediated by the external wedge of imported technology.
*   **Paragraph 1.2: The Continuity Hinge**

    *   *Focus:* Situate this chapter as the continuation of Chapter 1's finding that US cointegration survives only under distributional conditioning (rate of exploitation $e_t$).     *   *Name:* Okishio-Vidal Synthesis

    *   *Name:* Center-Periphery Relational Asymmetry
    *   *Focus:* Detail the comparative dimension: US internal mediation vs. Chile's peripheral constraint. Introduce the "peripheral wedge" (technological dependency and balance-of-payments constraints) that dictates capacity formation in the Global South.

### Section 2: The Micro-Meso Hinge: Okishio, Vidal, and the Contradictions of Capacity Formation

#### Subsection 2.1: Decentralized Accumulation and Market Anarchy (Okishio)
*   **Paragraph 2.1.1:** *Name: Anarchy of Investment*
    *   *Focus:* Define Okishio's concept of decentralized investment, where decisions are made without ex-ante coordination and validated only ex-post in the market.
*   **Paragraph 2.1.2:** *Name: Sectoral Disproportionality*
    *   *Focus:* Detail how uncoordinated accumulation leads to structural disproportionality and cumulative disequilibrium across capital-goods and consumer-goods sectors.
*   **Paragraph 2.1.3:** *Name: The Failure of Price Signals*
    *   *Focus:* Analyze why market price signals arrive too late to coordinate sunk capital commitments, resulting in chronic idle capacity as a systemic outcome.
*   **Paragraph 2.1.4:** *Name: The Unified-Firm Gap*
    *   *Focus:* Identify the conceptual gap in Okishio's macro framework: the implicit assumption that firms respond as coherent optimizing units, ignoring internal organizational limits.

#### Subsection 2.2: The Internally Divided Firm (Vidal)
*   **Paragraph 2.2.1:** *Name: The Valorization-Labor Process Contradiction*
    *   *Focus:* Frame the firm as an internally divided social organization, where the extraction of surplus-value (valorization) conflicts with the cooperative coordination of production.
*   **Paragraph 2.2.2:** *Name: Contradictory Managerial Imperatives*
    *   *Focus:* Detail the tension between managerial demands for labor standardization/intensity and the reliance on worker discretion/problem-solving.
*   **Paragraph 2.2.3:** *Name: Labor Resistance and Effort Calibration*
    *   *Focus:* Analyze how workers respond to contradictory management by quietly calibrating their effort and cooperation, directly affecting potential machine speeds.
*   **Paragraph 2.2.4:** *Name: Contested Capacity Conventions*
    *   *Focus:* Explain how "capacity" is not a neutral physical index but a contested accounting convention, resulting in firm-level settlements of "mediocre sufficiency."

#### Subsection 2.3: Synthesis and Process Tracing: The Productive-Capacity Corridor
*   **Paragraph 2.3.1:** *Name: Reinterpreting the Mechanization Frontier*
    *   *Focus:* Synthesize Okishio and Vidal. Reframe the mechanization frontier from a firm-level optimization problem into a macro-structural envelope of historical class conflict. Introduce stylized baseline parameters ($g_k = 3\%$, $g_{yp} = 2\%$, $\theta = 0.8$) and define the capital acceleration term ($d\hat{k}/dt$) to ground the capacity equations.
*   **Paragraph 2.3.2:** *Name: The Productive-Capacity Corridor*
    *   *Focus:* Define the productive-capacity corridor, linking distributional conditioning to the transformation elasticity ($\theta_t$), realized capacity utilization ($\mu_t$), and the profit rate ($r_t$) via the Weisskopf decomposition ($r_t = \mu_t b_t \pi_t$).
*   **Paragraph 2.3.3:** *Name: Bridging Micro Contradiction to Macro Disequilibrium*
    *   *Focus:* Summarize how shop-floor struggles over effort calibration and contested capacity conventions scale up to establish the aggregate capacity ceiling ($\hat{Y}_t^p$), explicitly noting that the feedback of this capacity ceiling onto long-run capital accumulation and dynamic investment functions ($\chi_t \rightarrow k_t \rightarrow g_t$) is deferred to Chapter 3.

### Section 3: The Comparative Extension: Center-Periphery Asymmetry and the External Wedge

#### Subsection 3.1: Internal Mediation in the Core (The US Case)
*   **Paragraph 3.1.1:** *Name: Institutionalized Class Compromise*
    *   *Focus:* Contextualize post-war US accumulation, showing how stable collective bargaining mediated the distribution of productivity gains.
*   **Paragraph 3.1.2:** *Name: Capital Composition and Scale Adjustments*
    *   *Focus:* Explain how US firms adjusted their capital scale and composition (machinery vs. structures) in response to domestic wage pressures.
*   **Paragraph 3.1.3:** *Name: Domestic Capacity Buffers and Financialization*
    *   *Focus:* Analyze how the US domestic financial setting permitted firms to maintain large capacity and inventory buffers to absorb realization crises.

#### Subsection 3.2: The Peripheral Wedge in the Global South (The Chilean Case)
*   **Paragraph 3.2.1:** *Name: Technological Dependency*
    *   *Focus:* Detail Chile's structural dependency on imported capital goods, meaning capacity expansion cannot be generated through domestic accumulation alone.
*   **Paragraph 3.2.2:** *Name: The Balance-of-Payments Constraint*
    *   *Focus:* Frame Chile's accumulation cycle as bounded by foreign exchange availability, which fluctuates with commodity export cycles (copper).
*   **Paragraph 3.2.3:** *Name: Non-Reinvestment of Surplus and Financial Hoarding*
    *   *Focus:* Explain the "non-reinvestment problem," where peripheral firms hoard surplus in liquid financial forms due to imported machinery bottlenecks.
*   **Paragraph 3.2.4:** *Name: Balance of Payments as Transmission to the Shop Floor*
    *   *Focus:* Trace how external balance of payments shocks translate into domestic wage suppression and heightened labor resistance on the shop floor.

#### Subsection 3.3: Differentiated Capacity-Formation Regimes
*   **Paragraph 3.3.1:** *Name: Beyond National-Container Logic*
    *   *Focus:* Reject the comparative method that treats center and periphery as isolated cases. Frame them as relational parts of a single global system.
*   **Paragraph 3.3.2:** *Name: Co-determination of the Global Division of Labor*
    *   *Focus:* Explain how the core's technological control and the periphery's primary export specialization are mutually reinforcing.
*   **Paragraph 3.3.3:** *Name: The Joint Breakdown of the 1970s Settlements*
    *   *Focus:* Analyze how the breakdown of Fordism in the US and the crisis of Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI) in Chile were chronologically and structurally linked.

### Section 4: Empirical Strategy and Identification Framework

#### Subsection 4.1: Observed and Recovered Objects
*   **Paragraph 4.1.1:** *Name: The Latent Capacity Ceiling*
    *   *Focus:* Establish that potential capacity is unobserved and must be recovered from the long-run relation between observed variables (output, capital, and distribution).
*   **Paragraph 4.1.2:** *Name: The Long-Run Identification Sequence*
    *   *Focus:* Formalize the step-by-step recovery sequence: cointegrating vector $\rightarrow \hat{\theta}_t \rightarrow \hat{Y}_t^p \rightarrow \hat{\mu}_t$.
*   **Paragraph 4.1.3:** *Name: Super-Consistency and Cointegration Limits*
    *   *Focus:* Argue that while super-consistency shields the long-run parameter from endogeneity bias under integration, it does not guarantee the economic consistency of the recovered object.

#### Subsection 4.2: Econometric Specification and Interactions
*   **Paragraph 4.2.1:** *Name: US Scale-Conditioning Specification (Specification A)*
    *   *Focus:* Present the log-level interaction specification testing if distribution conditions capacity directly through capital scale: $y_t = \alpha + \theta_0 k_t + \phi \tilde{d}_t + \theta_1 (k_t \tilde{d}_t)$.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.2:** *Name: US Composition-Mediated Specification (Specification B)*
    *   *Focus:* Present the alternative specification testing if distribution acts through capital composition (machinery vs. structures ratio, $\tau_t$): $y_t = \alpha + \theta k_t + \psi \tau_t + \phi \tilde{d}_t + \lambda (\tau_t \tilde{d}_t)$.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.3:** *Name: Chilean Peripheral Extension Specification*
    *   *Focus:* Present the extended specification incorporating the external wedge ($z_t$): $y_t = \alpha + \beta_1 k_t + \beta_2 (\omega_t k_t) + \beta_3 (z_t k_t) + \beta_4 (\omega_t z_t k_t)$.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.4:** *Name: Interaction Terms as Friction Proxies*
    *   *Focus:* Interpret the interaction terms not as demand-regime indicators, but as structural measures of organizational friction (Vidal) and external bottlenecks.

#### Subsection 4.3: Estimator Hierarchy and Normalization
*   **Paragraph 4.3.1:** *Name: FM-OLS as Preferred Estimator*
    *   *Focus:* Justify FM-OLS as the primary estimator due to its ability to correct for endogeneity and serial correlation in log-level regressions.
*   **Paragraph 4.3.2:** *Name: IM-OLS Robustness and DOLS Exclusion Rationale*
    *   *Focus:* Justify the use of IM-OLS as a check and explain why DOLS is excluded (its differencing dynamics insert non-structural terms that distort $\theta_t$).
*   **Paragraph 4.3.3:** *Name: Normalization and Level Anchoring (Pinch Year)*
    *   *Focus:* Explain the role of the pinch year ($\mu_{t_0} = 1$) to anchor the utilization level, and outline sensitivity tests across alternative peak-utilization years.
*   **Paragraph 4.3.4:** *Name: Econometric Admissibility and Pedagogical Counterfactuals*
    *   *Focus:* Explicitly define econometric admissibility as the survival of cointegration under structural distribution. Schedule two mandatory counterfactuals: (1) the Restriction Counterfactual ($\theta = 1.0$) to demonstrate the economic absurdity of imposing balanced growth, and (2) the No-Dummy Counterfactual to prove that omitting structural breaks generates spurious cointegration.

#### Subsection 4.4: Data Construction and Harmonization
*   **Paragraph 4.4.1:** *Name: US BEA Source Pathways and Descriptive Grounding*
    *   *Focus:* Detail the construction of US corporate sector variables from BEA NIPA and Fixed Asset Tables, emphasizing the separation of structures ($K^{NR}$) and machinery ($K^{ME}$). Emulate Stephen Marglin by integrating raw, non-econometric growth rates of GDP, employment, and capital (1947–2011) directly into the text to ground the econometrics.
*   **Paragraph 4.4.2:** *Name: Chile Capital Stock Harmonization*
    *   *Focus:* Detail the integration of the Pérez FBKF series (1900–1994) with ClioLab and Central Bank (BCCh) data.
*   **Paragraph 4.4.3:** *Name: Gross vs. Net Capital Stocks*
    *   *Focus:* Define the theoretical and empirical roles of gross capital stock (operating assets) and net capital stock (value dynamics) in the GPIM.
