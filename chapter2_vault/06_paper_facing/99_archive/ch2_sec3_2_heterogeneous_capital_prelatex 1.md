# Section 3.2: Heterogeneous Capital, the Mechanization Gap, and State-Dependent Viability

Mainstream macroeconomics treats the capital stock as a homogeneous aggregate, erasing the internal architecture of the firm. Following Vidal’s (2015, 2019) theory of the divided firm, we unbundle aggregate productive capital into extensive Nonresidential Structures (KNRCKNRC) and intensive Machinery (KMEKME). These components do not enter the labor process symmetrically. Structures form the passive infrastructure envelope—the factory floor and logistics shell. Machinery is the active, revolutionary force that restructures the production of relative surplus value (Marx, Vol. I, Ch. 15).

## 3.2.1 The Extensive Envelope and the Intensive Margin

Nonresidential structures (KNRCKNRC) define the **extensive margin** of accumulation. By structural design, KNRCKNRC evolves independently of short-run distributive conflict (ωω) and serves exclusively as the fixed envelope that conditions the parameters of the mechanization productive frontier. It evolves along a normal accumulation corridor governed by long-run reproduction conditions and institutional closure:

gKNRC,tN=χNrtN−δgKNRC,tN​=χNrtN​−δ

This extensive envelope Γ(KNRC)Γ(KNRC) sets the geometric boundary within which tactical technique choice occurs.

Given this fixed infrastructural envelope, the **intensive margin** of accumulation is the tactical choice of the mechanization gap, defined in natural logs as:

κt=ktME−ktNRCκt​=ktME​−ktNRC​

This gap measures the intensity of active mechanization relative to the extensive plant scale. The capacity identity decomposes potential output into the extensive envelope and the intensive capacity yield ΨtΨt​ generated by the gap:

ytp=θNRCktNRC+Ψt(κt,ωt,ptκ)ytp​=θNRC​ktNRC​+Ψt​(κt​,ωt​,ptκ​)

The critical theoretical intervention is that ΨtΨt​ is not a fixed engineering constant. It is the outcome of a decentralized cost-minimization problem governed by the regime of accumulation.

## 3.2.2 Cost-Surplus Maximization and the Okishio Viability Yield

The firm chooses the mechanization gap κtκt​ to maximize the net viability surplus, subject to the regime-conditioned mechanization frontier Φ(κt)=λ0+λ1κt+λ2κt2Φ(κt​)=λ0​+λ1​κt​+λ2​κt2​.

However, the _realized_ capacity yield is governed by the **Marx-Okishio Viability Condition**: a cost-minimizing firm deploys a new technique only if it reduces unit costs at current distributive and price parameters. We formalize this as a cost-surplus maximization problem. The firm maximizes the gross engineering yield of the gap minus the effective distributive and price costs:

max⁡κt c(κt)=Φ(κt)−κt⋅MC(ωt,ptκ)maxκt​​ c(κt​)=Φ(κt​)−κt​⋅MC(ωt​,ptκ​)

where MC(ωt,ptκ)MC(ωt​,ptκ​) is the marginal viability cost of the gap. This cost is determined by two channels:

1. **The Distributive Dividend (Labor Saving):** Increasing κtκt​ substitutes machinery for labor. The value of this displacement is strictly proportional to the wage share (ωtωt​). A higher wage share _reduces_ the net cost of mechanization, as the firm saves more on wages.
2. **The Relative Price Penalty (Capital Cost):** Increasing κtκt​ requires accumulating machinery faster than structures. The cost of this composition shift is proportional to the log relative price of machinery (ptκ=ln⁡PtME−ln⁡PtNRCptκ​=lnPtME​−lnPtNRC​). A higher relative price _increases_ the marginal cost of the gap.

We specify the marginal viability cost as:

MC(ωt,ptκ)=γ0−γ1ωt+γ2ptκMC(ωt​,ptκ​)=γ0​−γ1​ωt​+γ2​ptκ​

where γ1>0γ1​>0 captures the wage-savings dividend and γ2>0γ2​>0 captures the relative price penalty. Substituting the frontier and the cost function into the objective yields the unconstrained tactical problem:

c(κt)=λ0+[λ1−(γ0−γ1ωt+γ2ptκ)]κt+λ2κt2c(κt​)=λ0​+[λ1​−(γ0​−γ1​ωt​+γ2​ptκ​)]κt​+λ2​κt2​

**First-Order Condition:** Differentiating with respect to κtκt​ and setting to zero:

dcdκt=λ1−γ0+γ1ωt−γ2ptκ+2λ2κt∗=0dκt​dc​=λ1​−γ0​+γ1​ωt​−γ2​ptκ​+2λ2​κt∗​=0

**Optimal Mechanization Gap:**

κt∗(ωt,ptκ)=(γ0−λ1)−γ1ωt+γ2ptκ2λ2κt∗​(ωt​,ptκ​)=2λ2​(γ0​−λ1​)−γ1​ωt​+γ2​ptκ​​

Strict concavity (2λ2<02λ2​<0) guarantees a unique maximum. The comparative statics establish the induced innovation mechanism:

∂κt∗∂ωt=−γ12λ2>0∂ωt​∂κt∗​​=−2λ2​γ1​​>0 ∂κt∗∂ptκ=γ22λ2<0∂ptκ​∂κt∗​​=2λ2​γ2​​<0

A rising wage share forces cost-minimizing firms to widen the mechanization gap, substituting machinery for labor. Conversely, a rising relative price of machinery suppresses the optimal gap, as the structural cost of the composition shift penalizes the mechanization program.

## 3.2.3 The Marginal Capacity Payoff and the Econometric Bridge

The macroeconomic mapping from the mechanization gap to potential capacity is given by the **Marginal Capacity Payoff** (MPκMPκ​), which is the derivative of the intensive capacity yield ΨtΨt​ with respect to the gap κtκt​.

By the envelope theorem, the marginal capacity payoff is exactly the net marginal viability yield evaluated at the optimum:

MPκ=∂Ψt∂κt=∂Φ∂κt−MC(ωt,ptκ)MPκ​=∂κt​∂Ψt​​=∂κt​∂Φ​−MC(ωt​,ptκ​)

Substituting the derivatives:

MPκ=(λ1+2λ2κt)−(γ0−γ1ωt+γ2ptκ)MPκ​=(λ1​+2λ2​κt​)−(γ0​−γ1​ωt​+γ2​ptκ​)

To map this to the aggregate capacity equation, we integrate the marginal payoff with respect to the gap κtκt​ (treating the slow-moving macroeconomic state variables ωtωt​ and ptκptκ​ as predetermined shifters). Defining the structural parameters θκθκ​, θωκ=γ1θωκ​=γ1​, and θκpκ=−γ2θκpκ​=−γ2​, the integration yields the intensive capacity yield function:

Ψt=θκκt+θωκ(ωtκt)+θκpκ(ptκκt)Ψt​=θκ​κt​+θωκ​(ωt​κt​)+θκpκ​(ptκ​κt​)

Substituting ΨtΨt​ back into the capacity decomposition recovers the structural capacity equation:

ytp=θNRCktNRC+θκκt+θωκ(ωtκt)+θκpκ(ptκκt)ytp​=θNRC​ktNRC​+θκ​κt​+θωκ​(ωt​κt​)+θκpκ​(ptκ​κt​)

This derivation proves that the interaction terms are not ad-hoc econometric additions; they are the direct macroeconomic footprint of the divided firm's Okishio viability constraint. The distributive pressure and relative price enter the equation because they fundamentally alter the marginal capacity payoff of the mechanization gap.
