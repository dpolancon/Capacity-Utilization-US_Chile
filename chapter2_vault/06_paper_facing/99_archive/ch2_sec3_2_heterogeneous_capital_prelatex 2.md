# Section 3.2: Heterogeneous Capital, the Mechanization Gap, and State-Dependent Viability

Mainstream macroeconomics treats the capital stock as a homogeneous aggregate, erasing the internal architecture of the firm. Following Vidal’s (2015, 2019) theory of the divided firm, we unbundle aggregate productive capital into extensive Nonresidential Structures ($K^{NRC}$) and intensive Machinery ($K^{ME}$). These components do not enter the labor process symmetrically. Structures form the passive infrastructure envelope—the factory floor and logistics shell. Machinery is the active, revolutionary force that restructures the production of relative surplus value (Marx, Vol. I, Ch. 15).

## 3.2.1 The Extensive Envelope and the Intensive Margin

Nonresidential structures ($K^{NRC}$) define the **extensive margin** of accumulation. By structural design, $K^{NRC}$ evolves independently of short-run distributive conflict ($\omega$) and serves exclusively as the fixed envelope that conditions the parameters of the mechanization productive frontier. It evolves along a normal accumulation corridor governed by long-run reproduction conditions and institutional closure:

$$ g_{K^{NRC}, t}^N = \chi^N r^N_t - \delta $$

This extensive envelope $\Gamma(K^{NRC})$ sets the geometric boundary within which tactical technique choice occurs.

Given this fixed infrastructural envelope, the **intensive margin** of accumulation is the tactical choice of the mechanization gap, defined in natural logs as:

$$ \kappa_t = k^{ME}_t - k^{NRC}_t $$

This gap measures the intensity of active mechanization relative to the extensive plant scale. The capacity identity decomposes potential output into the extensive envelope and the intensive capacity yield $\Psi_t$ generated by the gap:

$$ y^p_t = \theta_{NRC} k^{NRC}_t + \Psi_t(\kappa_t, \omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t) $$

The critical theoretical intervention is that $\Psi_t$ is not a fixed engineering constant. It is the outcome of a decentralized cost-minimization problem governed by the regime of accumulation.

## 3.2.2 Cost-Surplus Maximization and the Okishio Viability Yield

The firm chooses the mechanization gap $\kappa_t$ to maximize the net viability surplus, subject to the regime-conditioned mechanization frontier $\Phi(\kappa_t) = \lambda_0 + \lambda_1 \kappa_t + \lambda_2 \kappa_t^2$. 

However, the *realized* capacity yield is governed by the **Marx-Okishio Viability Condition**: a cost-minimizing firm deploys a new technique only if it reduces unit costs at current distributive and price parameters. We formalize this as a cost-surplus maximization problem. The firm maximizes the gross engineering yield of the gap minus the effective distributive and price costs:

$$ \max_{\kappa_t} \ c(\kappa_t) = \Phi(\kappa_t) - \kappa_t \cdot MC(\omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t) $$

where $MC(\omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t)$ is the marginal viability cost of the gap. This cost is determined by two channels:
1. **The Distributive Dividend (Labor Saving):** Increasing $\kappa_t$ substitutes machinery for labor. The value of this displacement is strictly proportional to the lagged wage share ($\omega_{t-1}$), reflecting the wage settlement period prior to current capacity realization. A higher wage share *reduces* the net cost of mechanization, as the firm saves more on wages.
2. **The Relative Price Penalty (Capital Cost):** Increasing $\kappa_t$ requires accumulating machinery faster than structures. The cost of this composition shift is proportional to the log relative price of machinery ($p^\kappa_t = \ln P^{ME}_t - \ln P^{NRC}_t$). A higher relative price *increases* the marginal cost of the gap.

We specify the marginal viability cost as:

$$ MC(\omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t) = \gamma_0 - \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} + \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t $$

where $\gamma_1 > 0$ captures the wage-savings dividend and $\gamma_2 > 0$ captures the relative price penalty. Substituting the frontier and the cost function into the objective yields the unconstrained tactical problem:

$$ c(\kappa_t) = \lambda_0 + \left[ \lambda_1 - (\gamma_0 - \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} + \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t) \right] \kappa_t + \lambda_2 \kappa_t^2 $$

**First-Order Condition:** Differentiating with respect to $\kappa_t$ and setting to zero:

$$ \frac{dc}{d\kappa_t} = \lambda_1 - \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} - \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t + 2\lambda_2 \kappa_t^* = 0 $$

**Optimal Mechanization Gap:**

$$ \kappa_t^*(\omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t) = \frac{(\gamma_0 - \lambda_1) - \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} + \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t}{2\lambda_2} $$

Strict concavity ($2\lambda_2 < 0$) guarantees a unique maximum. The comparative statics establish the induced innovation mechanism:

$$ \frac{\partial \kappa_t^*}{\partial \omega_{t-1}} = -\frac{\gamma_1}{2\lambda_2} > 0 $$
$$ \frac{\partial \kappa_t^*}{\partial p^\kappa_t} = \frac{\gamma_2}{2\lambda_2} < 0 $$

A rising wage share forces cost-minimizing firms to widen the mechanization gap, substituting machinery for labor. Conversely, a rising relative price of machinery suppresses the optimal gap, as the structural cost of the composition shift penalizes the mechanization program.

## 3.2.3 The Marginal Capacity Payoff and the Econometric Bridge

The macroeconomic mapping from the mechanization gap to potential capacity is given by the **Marginal Capacity Payoff** ($MP_\kappa$), which is the derivative of the intensive capacity yield $\Psi_t$ with respect to the gap $\kappa_t$. 

By the envelope theorem, the marginal capacity payoff is exactly the net marginal viability yield evaluated at the optimum:

$$ MP_\kappa = \frac{\partial \Psi_t}{\partial \kappa_t} = \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \kappa_t} - MC(\omega_{t-1}, p^\kappa_t) $$

Substituting the derivatives:

$$ MP_\kappa = (\lambda_1 + 2\lambda_2 \kappa_t) - (\gamma_0 - \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} + \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t) $$

Rearranging terms to isolate the state variables yields the state-dependent marginal product:

$$ MP_\kappa = \underbrace{(\lambda_1 - \gamma_0 + 2\lambda_2 \kappa_t)}_{\text{Autonomous Yield}} + \gamma_1 \omega_{t-1} - \gamma_2 p^\kappa_t $$

To map this to the aggregate capacity equation, we integrate the marginal payoff with respect to the gap $\kappa_t$ (treating the slow-moving macroeconomic state variables $\omega_{t-1}$ and $p^\kappa_t$ as predetermined shifters). Defining the structural parameters $\theta_\kappa$, $\theta_{\omega\kappa} = \gamma_1$, and $\theta_{\kappa p\kappa} = -\gamma_2$, the integration yields the intensive capacity yield function:

$$ \Psi_t = \theta_\kappa \kappa_t + \theta_{\omega\kappa} (\omega_{t-1} \kappa_t) + \theta_{\kappa p\kappa} (p^\kappa_t \kappa_t) $$

Substituting $\Psi_t$ back into the capacity decomposition recovers the structural capacity equation:

$$ y^p_t = \theta_{NRC} k^{NRC}_t + \theta_\kappa \kappa_t + \theta_{\omega\kappa} (\omega_{t-1} \kappa_t) + \theta_{\kappa p\kappa} (p^\kappa_t \kappa_t) $$

This derivation proves that the interaction terms are not ad-hoc econometric additions; they are the direct macroeconomic footprint of the divided firm's Okishio viability constraint. The distributive pressure and relative price enter the equation because they fundamentally alter the marginal capacity payoff of the mechanization gap.

## 3.2.4 The Aggregate Transformation Elasticity

The macroeconomic stability of the aggregate transformation elasticity ($\theta^{SpecB}_t$) depends entirely on this state-dependent mechanism. We define the aggregate elasticity as the ratio of the total differentials of potential capacity ($dy^p_t$) and aggregate log capital stock ($dk^{cap}_t$) as they vary jointly along the historical accumulation path.

Taking the total differential of the capacity equation (with respect to the capital components, holding the slow-moving state variables fixed), we explicitly distinguish the contribution of the extensive envelope from the intensive mechanization margin:

$$ dy^p_t = \frac{\partial y^p_t}{\partial k^{NRC}_t} dk^{NRC}_t + \frac{\partial y^p_t}{\partial \kappa_t} d\kappa_t $$

Substituting the partial derivatives derived above ($\partial y^p_t / \partial k^{NRC}_t = \theta_{NRC}$ and $\partial y^p_t / \partial \kappa_t = MP_\kappa$), this becomes:

$$ dy^p_t = \theta_{NRC} dk^{NRC}_t + MP_\kappa d\kappa_t $$
$$ dy^p_t = \theta_{NRC} dk^{NRC}_t + \left( \theta_\kappa + \theta_{\omega\kappa} \omega_{t-1} + \theta_{\kappa p\kappa} p^\kappa_t \right) d\kappa_t $$

Concurrently, the variation in the aggregate capital stock ($dk^{cap}_t$) must be carefully identified. Aggregate capital is the Divisia index of its heterogeneous components:

$$ dk^{cap}_t \approx s_{ME,t} dk^{ME}_t + s_{NRC,t} dk^{NRC}_t $$

Using the definition of the mechanization gap ($d\kappa_t = dk^{ME}_t - dk^{NRC}_t$) and noting that the nominal shares sum to unity ($s_{ME,t} + s_{NRC,t} = 1$), the variation in aggregate capital simplifies to:

$$ dk^{cap}_t \approx s_{ME,t} (d\kappa_t + dk^{NRC}_t) + s_{NRC,t} dk^{NRC}_t = dk^{NRC}_t + s_{ME,t} d\kappa_t $$

The aggregate transformation elasticity is the ratio of these total differentials. Dividing both the numerator and the denominator by the variation in the extensive envelope ($dk^{NRC}_t$) yields the elasticity evaluated along the empirical growth path of the mechanization gap ($\frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}$):

$$ \theta^{SpecB}_t = \frac{dy^p_t}{dk^{cap}_t} = \frac{\theta_{NRC} dk^{NRC}_t + MP_\kappa d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t + s_{ME,t} d\kappa_t} = \frac{\theta_{NRC} + MP_\kappa \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}}{1 + s_{ME,t} \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}} $$

Expanding the state-dependent marginal capacity payoff $MP_\kappa$, we obtain the final structural expression for the aggregate transformation elasticity:

$$ \theta^{SpecB}_t = \frac{\theta_{NRC} + \left( \theta_\kappa + \theta_{\omega\kappa} \omega_{t-1} + \theta_{\kappa p\kappa} p^\kappa_t \right) \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}}{1 + s_{ME,t} \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}} $$

> [!conclusion] The Overaccumulation Regime ($\theta_t^{SpecB} < 1$)
> Under **strong labor closure** (high $\omega_{t-1}$), the induced-efficiency parameter $\theta_{\omega\kappa}$ anchors the elasticity, ensuring that mechanization translates smoothly into capacity (see [[R_distribution_conditioned_theta_identification]]). 
> 
> Under **weak labor closure** and rising machinery costs, the state-dependent marginal viability yield collapses. This mechanically drives the aggregate transformation elasticity below unity:
> $$ \theta_t^{SpecB} < 1 $$
> This inequality formally defines the **Structural Overaccumulation Regime** (see [[A03_TransformationElasticity_Two-CapitalCapacityComposition]] and [[H01_Cajas_to_Transformation_Elasticity]]): a historical phase in which additional capital accumulation yields diminishing or negative capacity expansion, decoupling the intensive mechanization margin from the extensive structural envelope.

## 3.2.5 Empirical Identification Strategy for Heterogeneous Capital (Specification B)

Section 3.2.3 established the log-level potential output function for heterogeneous capital. Adding a stationary stochastic error term $u_t$ under the null hypothesis of cointegration yields the estimating equation for Specification B:

$$ y_t = \alpha + \theta_{NRC} k_t^{NRC} + \theta_\kappa \kappa_t + \theta_{\omega\kappa} \left( \omega_{t-1} \cdot \kappa_t \right) + \theta_{\kappa p\kappa} \left( p^\kappa_t \cdot \kappa_t \right) + u_t $$

where $y_t = \ln Y_t$ is log real corporate output, $k_t^{NRC}$ is log nonresidential structures, $\kappa_t = k_t^{ME} - k_t^{NRC}$ is the log mechanization gap, $\omega_{t-1}$ is the lagged wage share, and $p^\kappa_t = \ln P^{ME}_t - \ln P^{NRC}_t$ is the log relative price of machinery relative to structures.

### The Anti-Residual Distinction: Structural Recovery for Heterogeneous Capital

Following the locked capacity reconstruction protocol (`D03_capacity_utilization_level_anchor_pinch_year_protocol.md`), we explicitly reject defining capacity utilization ($\mu_t$) as the regression residual $\hat{u}_t$ or as a detrended output gap. The residual $\hat{u}_t$ represents short-run econometric noise under the cointegration null, not structural productive capacity. 

Instead, capacity utilization under Specification B is structurally rescued through a strict four-step downstream sequence:

1. **State-Dependent Elasticity Recovery:** Cointegration estimation recovers the structural parameter vector $(\hat{\theta}_{NRC}, \hat{\theta}_\kappa, \hat{\theta}_{\omega\kappa}, \hat{\theta}_{\kappa p\kappa})$. These estimates, together with the observed state variables ($\omega_{t-1}$, $p^\kappa_t$) and the empirical mechanization path ($d\kappa_t / dk^{NRC}_t$), are substituted into the aggregate elasticity formula derived in Section 3.2.4 to construct the state-dependent elasticity series:
   $$ \hat{\theta}_t^{SpecB} = \frac{\hat{\theta}_{NRC} + \left( \hat{\theta}_\kappa + \hat{\theta}_{\omega\kappa} \omega_{t-1} + \hat{\theta}_{\kappa p\kappa} p^\kappa_t \right) \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}}{1 + s_{ME,t} \frac{d\kappa_t}{dk^{NRC}_t}} $$
   This is the binding identification: $\hat{\theta}_t^{SpecB}$ is the total-derivative ratio $dy^p_t / dk^{cap}_t$, not merely its numerator.
2. **Dynamic Potential Capacity Path:** Potential capacity growth is constructed by applying the aggregate elasticity to the growth rate of aggregate productive capital ($g_{K^{cap},t} = \Delta \ln K^{cap}_t$):
   $$ g_{\hat{Y}^p, t} = \hat{\theta}_t^{SpecB} \cdot g_{K^{cap}, t} $$
   This identity is exact by construction: it follows directly from the definition $\hat{\theta}_t^{SpecB} \equiv dy^p_t / dk^{cap}_t$. Cumulating $g_{\hat{Y}^p, t}$ recovers the trajectory and slope of potential output $\hat{Y}^p_t$.
3. **Pinch-Year Level Anchoring:** Absolute level scale is anchored at the externally motivated full-capacity reference year ($t^*_{US} = 1973$), setting:
   $$ \hat{\mu}_{1973} = 1 \quad \implies \quad \hat{Y}^p_{1973} = Y_{1973} $$
4. **Aggregate Macro Extrapolation:** Capacity utilization is extrapolated across the full historical sample using **aggregate observed real output ($Y_t = \text{GDP}_t$)**:
   $$ \hat{\mu}_t = \frac{Y_t^{\text{GDP}}}{\hat{Y}^p_t} $$
   Evaluating against macro Real GDP ($Y_t$) ensures that realization discipline reflects economy-wide aggregate demand constraints.

### Core Heterogeneous Capital Hypotheses

Specification B generates three testable empirical propositions regarding the U.S. corporate sector:

* **Hypothesis 1 (The Okishio Distributive Dividend):** $\hat{\theta}_{\omega\kappa} > 0$. Distributive wage pressure structurally increases the capacity-generating yield of the mechanization gap.
* **Hypothesis 2 (The Relative Price Penalty):** $\hat{\theta}_{\kappa p\kappa} < 0$. Relative price inflation in machinery penalizes the intensive margin, reducing the capacity payoff of mechanization.
* **Hypothesis 3 (The Structural Overaccumulation Regime):** Under weak labor closure (post-Fordism), the collapse of the state-dependent marginal viability yield drives the aggregate transformation elasticity below unity ($\theta_t^{SpecB} < 1$), producing chronic industrial stagnation.
