# Chapter 2 Definitive Outline & Paragraph Micro-Outline

## I. Foundational Metadata & Context

*   **Chapter Title:** Internally Divided Firm and Market Anarchy: Growth and Distribution in Center and Periphery
*   **Relationship to Chapter 1:** Direct continuation. Chapter 1 demonstrated that the US aggregate output–capital relation fails to cointegrate in isolation, requiring the rate of exploitation ($e_t$) in a trivariate VECM to establish stability and yielding a long-run transformation elasticity $\hat{\theta} < 1.0$ (structural overaccumulation). Chapter 2 provides the micro-meso foundations for this distributional mediation and extends it comparatively to Chile's peripheral formation.
*   **Relationship to Vault Analytical Foundations:** Integrates the formal analytical models from `chapter2_vault/02_analyitical_foundation` (A00–A05, H00–H01). The baseline US model and its peripheral Chilean extension deploy formal mathematical statements for the extensive margin envelope ($K^{NRC}$), intensive margin mechanization choice ($K^{ME}$), two-capital capacity composition identity, and foreign-exchange (FX) realization bottlenecks. All formal model statements, first-order conditions, and solutions in this outline represent smooth reproduction benchmarks ("abstractions of not becoming"); their formal statements and final solutions remain pending audit and definitive versioning.
*   **Writing Calibration:** Styled in the authoritative, active-voice register of Chapter 1. Complies with `/umass-applied-econometrics` (BLUF by second paragraph, non-parametric visualization first, endogeneity/distribution as endogenous drivers, specification-space mapping, descriptive macro grounding, institutional critique) and `/humanize-writing` (no AI transitions, active voice $\ge 70\%$, passive voice $< 30\%$, hedging density $< 5\%$, sentence length variety) principles.

#### Subsection 2.3: The Formal Baseline Analytical Model (US / Core Case)
> [!NOTE]
> *Audit Status:* The formal model statements, first-order conditions, and solutions presented in this subsection represent baseline analytical benchmarks ("abstractions of not becoming" under smooth reproduction per `H00` and `R05`). Their final mathematical statements and solutions remain pending audit and definitive versioning.

*   **Paragraph 2.3.1:** *Name: Ontological Guardrail — Benchmark of "Not Becoming" vs Historical "Becoming"*
    *   *Focus:* Synthesize Okishio and Vidal with the analytical guardrail in `H00`. Clarify that the optimization over technique does not assume a representative, profit-maximizing firm solving a frictionless problem. Instead, formal optimization yields the smooth-reproduction benchmark ($a^N, q^*$, "abstraction of not becoming"), while historical realization ("becoming") is scarred by market anarchy (Okishio) and shop-floor resistance (Vidal).
*   **Paragraph 2.3.2:** *Name: The Extensive Margin ($K^{NRC}$ Envelope)*
    *   *Focus:* Formalize the extensive margin (`A01`). Nonresidential construction and infrastructure ($K^{NRC}$) define the physical plant scale and logistical envelope $\Gamma(K^{NRC})$. $K^{NRC}$ evolves along a normal reproduction corridor ($g_{K^{NRC}, t}^N = \chi^N r_t^N - \delta$) driven by long-run institutional closure. Establish the independence property ($\frac{\partial K^{NRC}}{\partial \omega} = 0$), showing that multi-year plant commitments filter out short-run wage bargaining ($\omega$) as cyclical noise.
*   **Paragraph 2.3.3:** *Name: The Intensive Margin (Technique Choice and Mechanization)*
    *   *Focus:* Formalize the tactical choice of technique (`A02`, `H01`). Within the structural envelope, firms choose mechanization intensity $q = K^{ME}/L$ (machinery per worker) to maximize net productivity surplus: $\max_q c(q) = a - q\pi$, where $\pi = 1-\omega$ is the nominal profit share, subject to the regime-conditioned technological frontier $a = \Phi(q; K^{NRC}) = \lambda_0^{[s]} + \lambda_1^{[s]} q + \lambda_2^{[s]} q^2$ ($\lambda_2^{[s]} < 0$). Derive the First-Order Condition (FOC): $q^*(\pi, s) = \frac{\pi - \lambda_1^{[s]}}{2\lambda_2^{[s]}}$. Prove the induced innovation comparative statics ($\frac{\partial q^*}{\partial \pi} = \frac{1}{2\lambda_2^{[s]}} < 0 \implies$ rising wage share $\omega \uparrow \implies \pi \downarrow \implies q^* \uparrow$). Define normal labor productivity $a^N(\pi, s) = \lambda_0^{[s]} + \lambda_1^{[s]} q^* + \lambda_2^{[s]} (q^*)^2$.
*   **Paragraph 2.3.4:** *Name: Two-Capital Capacity Composition and Transformation Elasticity*
    *   *Focus:* Derive the aggregate capacity identity (`A03`): $Y^p = \frac{a^N(\pi, s)}{q^*(\pi, s)} K^{ME} + \Gamma(K^{NRC})$. Define the machinery capacity payoff $\theta^{ME}(\pi, s) = \frac{a^N}{q^*} = \frac{\lambda_0^{[s]}}{q^*} + \lambda_1^{[s]} + \lambda_2^{[s]} q^*$ and the structural payoff $\theta^{NRC} = \frac{\partial \ln \Gamma(K^{NRC})}{\partial \ln K^{NRC}}$. Derive the composition-weighted transformation elasticity $\theta^N(\pi, s) = \frac{\theta^{NRC}(1-s)g_{K^{NRC}} + \theta^{ME}(\pi, s) s g_{K^{ME}}}{(1-s)g_{K^{NRC}} + s g_{K^{ME}}}$, where $s = K^{ME}/(K^{ME} + K^{NRC})$ is the physical composition share in base-year constant reference prices ($P_r^j = 1$). Recover the traditional productivity-mechanization gap $b = a^N - q^*$ as the machinery-dominated limiting case ($s \to 1 \implies \theta^N \approx 1 + \frac{b}{g_K^N}$, per `H01`).
*   **Paragraph 2.3.5:** *Name: Scaling Micro Contradiction to Macro Disequilibrium*
    *   *Focus:* Trace how shop-floor effort calibration (Vidal) and market anarchy (Okishio) push historical productivity ($a_t$) away from $a^N$, generating observed capacity utilization ($\mu_t = Y_t / Y_t^p \neq 1$) and profit rate ceilings ($r_t = \mu_t b_t \pi_t$). Explicitly state that the feedback of this capacity ceiling onto dynamic recapitalization ($\chi_t \rightarrow k_t \rightarrow g_t$) is deferred to Chapter 3.

#### Subsection 3.2: The Peripheral Wedge and Foreign-Exchange Bottleneck (The Chilean Case)
> [!NOTE]
> *Audit Status:* The formal peripheral model statements, conversion ratios, and kinked elasticity solutions in this subsection represent baseline analytical benchmarks (`A04`). Their final mathematical statements remain pending audit and definitive versioning.

*   **Paragraph 3.2.1:** *Name: Technological Dependency and Import-Constrained Machinery*
    *   *Focus:* Detail Chile's structural dependency on imported capital goods. Show that machinery investment and maintenance require imported components: $i^{ME}_t = i^{ME, dom}_t + i^{ME, imp}_t$, with a foreign exchange requirement $F^{req}_t = \mu_{imp} P^{ME, imp}_t i^{ME}_t$.
*   **Paragraph 3.2.2:** *Name: The Foreign-Exchange Conversion Ratio*
    *   *Focus:* Formalize the foreign-exchange realization bottleneck (`A04`). Available foreign exchange $F^{avail}_t$ (exports, capital flows, reserves net of debt service) gates the conversion of accounting surplus into real machinery accumulation via the conversion ratio $\phi_t = \min\left(1, \frac{F^{avail}_t}{F^{req}_t}\right)$.
*   **Paragraph 3.2.3:** *Name: Effective Profit Share Squeeze and Blocked Surplus*
    *   *Focus:* Define the effective profit share available for reinvestment: $\pi_t^{eff} = \pi_t \cdot \phi_t$. Show that when foreign exchange scarcity binds ($\phi_t < 1$), accounting profits are blocked ($1 - \phi_t$) from becoming productive capital, squeezing realized machinery growth ($g_{K^{ME}} = \chi^{ME} \pi^{eff} - \delta^{ME}$) independently of domestic wage bargaining ($\omega_t$).
*   **Paragraph 3.2.4:** *Name: Kinked Peripheral Transformation Elasticity*
    *   *Focus:* Define the two peripheral regimes: Regime $E_A$ (FX abundant, $\phi_t = 1 \implies \pi^{eff} = \pi$) vs. Regime $E_B$ (FX scarce, $\phi_t < 1 \implies \pi^{eff} < \pi$), where the realized machinery elasticity suffers a peripheral efficiency penalty: $\theta_{periph}^{ME} = \theta^{ME}(\pi^{eff}, s) \cdot \kappa(\phi_t)$. Show how external payment shocks translate into domestic wage suppression and heightened shop-floor friction.

### Section 4: Empirical Strategy and Identification Framework

#### Subsection 4.1: Observed and Recovered Objects
*   **Paragraph 4.1.1:** *Name: The Latent Capacity Ceiling*
    *   *Focus:* Establish that potential capacity ($Y^p$) is unobserved and must be recovered from the long-run relation between observed variables (output, capital, and distribution).
*   **Paragraph 4.1.2:** *Name: The Long-Run Identification Sequence*
    *   *Focus:* Formalize the step-by-step recovery sequence (`A00`): long-run cointegrating vector $\rightarrow \hat{\theta}_t \rightarrow \hat{Y}_t^p \rightarrow \hat{\mu}_t$.
*   **Paragraph 4.1.3:** *Name: Super-Consistency and Integration-Order Admissibility*
    *   *Focus:* Argue that while super-consistency shields the long-run parameter from endogeneity bias under integration, it does not guarantee economic consistency. Explicitly define the pre-estimation integration order gate: base variables must be classified as $I(1)$, dictating how the resulting $I(2)$ trap of interacted variables must be managed.

#### Subsection 4.2: Econometric Specifications and Interactions
*   **Paragraph 4.2.1:** *Name: The Raw Interaction in CPR IM-OLS (Prohibition of FWL Pre-Filtering)*
    *   *Focus:* Detail the baseline specification rule. Pre-estimation FWL orthogonalization is formally forbidden because partial summation does not commute with projection ($S M_X \neq M_{SX} S$), altering the Brownian limit functional. The cross-product enters raw as an $O_p(T)$ degree-2 polynomial CPR regressor in the full IM-OLS design. Numerical conditioning and structural interpretability are governed by the Three-Regime Centering Architecture (Regime A Uncentered Main, Regime B Initial-State Primary Robustness).
*   **Paragraph 4.2.2:** *Name: US Scale-Conditioning Specification (Specification A)*
    *   *Focus:* Present the log-level baseline specification (`A00`, `A05`) testing if distribution conditions capacity directly through aggregate productive capital scale ($k_t \equiv \ln K_t^{NRC}$): $y_t = \alpha + b k_t + f \omega_t + d (k_t \omega_{t-1}) + u_t$, estimated via IM-OLS partial sum transformation.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.3:** *Name: US Composition-Mediated Specification (Specification B)*
    *   *Focus:* Present the alternative specification (`A03`, `A05`) testing if distribution acts through productive-capital composition: $y_t = \alpha + b k_t + \psi \tau_t + f \omega_t + d (\tau_t \omega_{t-1}) + u_t$, where $\tau_t \equiv \ln K_t^{ME} - \ln K_t^{NRC}$ is the capital composition log difference.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.4:** *Name: Chilean Peripheral Extension Specification (Specification C)*
    *   *Focus:* Present the extended specification incorporating the external wedge ($z_t$, proxying foreign exchange availability $\phi_t$ per `A04`). The 3-way external bottleneck interaction presents a unique econometric challenge documented in `A06`.
*   **Paragraph 4.2.5:** *Name: Structural Interaction Terms as Friction Proxies*
    *   *Focus:* Interpret the structural interaction terms not as demand-regime indicators, but as quantitative measures of organizational friction (Vidal) and external realization bottlenecks (Prebisch/Thirlwall).

#### Subsection 4.3: Estimator Hierarchy and Normalization
*   **Paragraph 4.3.1:** *Name: IM-OLS (CPR-adapted) as the Preferred Active Baseline*
    *   *Focus:* Justify Integrated Modified OLS (IM-OLS), specifically adapted for Cointegrating Polynomial Regressions (CPR) via partial sum transformation, as the baseline estimator. It is robust to the integration-order complexities introduced by structural interaction terms.
*   **Paragraph 4.3.2:** *Name: FM-OLS as Diagnostic Comparator and DOLS Exclusion*
    *   *Focus:* Explain that FM-OLS is demoted to a diagnostic comparator due to its sensitivity to $I(2)$ long-run covariance matrix estimation. Maintain the rationale for excluding DOLS entirely from the baseline (severe regressor explosion and collinearity).
*   **Paragraph 4.3.3:** *Name: Normalization and Level Anchoring (Pinch Year)*
    *   *Focus:* Explain the role of the pinch year ($\mu_{t_0} = 1$) to anchor the utilization level, and outline sensitivity tests across alternative peak-utilization years.
*   **Paragraph 4.3.4:** *Name: Econometric Admissibility and Pedagogical Counterfactuals*
    *   *Focus:* Explicitly define econometric admissibility as the survival of cointegration under structural distribution. Schedule two mandatory counterfactuals: (1) the Restriction Counterfactual ($\theta = 1.0$) to demonstrate the economic absurdity of imposing balanced growth, and (2) the No-Dummy Counterfactual to prove that omitting structural breaks generates spurious cointegration.
