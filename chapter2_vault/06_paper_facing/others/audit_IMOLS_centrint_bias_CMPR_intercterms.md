# Econometric Audit: IM-OLS Centering Bias in CMPR Interaction Terms

**Verdict Summary**
Centering the distributive conflict variable ($\omega$) at its initial condition, or orthogonalizing the interaction term ($k \cdot \omega$) against the base term ($k$), introduces strictly zero econometric bias to the estimated transformation elasticity ($\theta_K$), the long-run variance, or the fixed-$b$ non-standard critical values, provided the base term $k$ is retained in the full cointegrating regression. The Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) theorem and the strict linearity of the partial sum operator guarantee that any location shift in the interaction multiplier $\omega$ is completely absorbed by the base coefficient $\beta_k$, leaving the parameter of interest $\beta_{k \cdot \omega}$ and its corresponding residuals mathematically invariant. The observed proximity of $\theta_K$ to theoretical expectations reflects the underlying structural cointegrating relationship in the data, not a statistical artifact of the centering transformation.

**Algebraic Invariance of the IM-OLS Estimator**
The IM-OLS estimator identifies the cointegrating vector by applying Ordinary Least Squares to the partial sums of the dependent and independent variables. Because the partial sum operator is strictly linear ($S_{aX + bY} = aS_X + bS_Y$), any affine transformation of the interaction term prior to summation maps linearly into the partial sum space. Furthermore, the Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) orthogonalization applied in Set 1 ($\omega \cdot k \sim a + b \cdot k$) and Set 2 ($(1-\iota) \cdot \omega \cdot k \sim a + b \cdot k$) yields mathematically identical residual vectors. The projection operator $M_k = I - k(k'k)^{-1}k'$ annihilates any component perfectly collinear with the base term $k$. Since the centered interaction $(1-\iota) \cdot \omega \cdot k$ simplifies to $\omega \cdot k - c \cdot k$ (where $c$ is the centering scalar), projecting this centered term onto $k$ returns the exact same residual as projecting the raw interaction $\omega \cdot k$ onto $k$ ($M_k(\omega \cdot k - c \cdot k) = M_k(\omega \cdot k)$). When the full Cointegrating Multivariate Polynomial Regression (CMPR) specification includes the base level $k$, the OLS estimator projects the partial sum of the dependent variable onto an identical vector space, guaranteeing that the estimated coefficient on the interaction term remains exactly unchanged across all pre-orthogonalization strategies. The location shift is perfectly isolated within the base coefficient $\beta_k$.

**Residual Stability and Fixed-b Inference**
Centering imposes no distortion on the fixed-$b$ structural break tests or the $CT_{IM}$ global tests because the regression residuals are algebraically identical under this linear reparameterization. Since the projection space remains unchanged, the sequence of estimated residuals $\hat{u}_t$ matches exactly across both the raw and centered specifications. Consequently, the long-run variance estimator $\hat{\Omega}_{uu}$—which scales the fixed-$b$ test statistics—and the Bai-Perron endogenous break algorithm process the exact same residual vector. The non-standard critical bounds simulated under the specification-dependent Monte Carlo DGP (as mandated by the `cpr-im-ols` protocol) remain fully valid and identically calibrated; the simulated stochastic processes $\omega^*$ and $k^*$ undergo the same FWL projection, ensuring the empirical test statistics map flawlessly to the $H_0$ distribution without size distortion or power loss.

**Omitted Variable Vulnerability**
Centering introduces asymptotic bias and invalidates fixed-$b$ inference only if the base term $k$ is omitted from the cointegrating vector ($x$). Omission forces the interaction parameter $\beta_{k \cdot \omega}$ to erroneously absorb the direct capital effect, violating the structural CMPR design and causing the transformation elasticity to depend arbitrarily on the initial condition. Strict adherence to the `cpr-im-ols` protocol—which rigorously partitions variables into $x\_base$ and $x$ to ensure all base stochastic levels are retained alongside their polynomials—prevents this omitted variable bias, confirming the centered specification as a robust, mathematically sound identification of the transformation elasticity.
