# H02: Peripheral Divided Management and World-System Entanglement

## Core Claim

The internal firm contradiction between valorization and the labor process ([[H00_Okishio_Vidal_Decentralized_Accumulation]]) acquires a specific institutional form in peripheral capitalism. Foreign exchange (FX) scarcity and technological dependence on imported machinery ($K^{ME}$) prevent the automatic reinvestment of accounting surplus into physical capacity ([[02_analyitical_foundation/_legacy/A04_PeripheralTransformationElasticity]]). 

This **non-reinvestment of surplus** channels balance-of-payments (BoP) pressures directly into shop-floor labor relations: management demands wage suppression and work intensification without being able to deliver physical plant expansion or productivity-enhancing equipment. The peripheral capacity utilization configuration is the institutional settlement through which firms manage the concrete contradiction between technological dependence and financial constraint.

---

## 1. The Non-Reinvestment Mechanism inside the Peripheral Firm

In core economies, managerial cost-containment can present a coherent accumulation narrative to labor: productivity gains through machinery investment ($K^{ME}$) offset wage restraint. In the periphery under Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI):

1. Advanced capital goods cannot be produced domestically.
2. Accumulation requires importing $K^{ME}$, gated by FX availability ($F^{avail}_t$).
3. When BoP constraints bind ($\phi_t < 1$), accounting profits cannot be converted into physical equipment.
4. Capitalists hold surplus in liquid/financial assets as hedges against import bottlenecks.

**Workplace Impact:** Management demands wage suppression and labor discipline to protect margins under macro instability, but cannot provide the capital equipment needed to ease workload or raise productivity. Labor responds by withholding discretionary effort and problem-solving, exacerbating operational bottlenecks.

---

## 2. State-Mediated Capacity Settlements

Developmental state institutions (exchange controls, tariff protection, directed credit, development banks like CORFO) emerge to mediate the market's structural inability to allocate capital goods.

Perennial terms-of-trade deterioration (Prebisch 1981) creates **technological dependency**: the periphery must export more commodities to import the same volume of core machinery. Peripheral capacity utilization configurations reflect state-managed settlements that are periodically shattered by external shocks.

---

## 3. Comparative-Relational Crisis Entanglement

Core and peripheral capitalisms do not operate on different economic logics. Both operate on the **same logic**—decentralized investment anarchy, divided management, labor resistance—under structurally differentiated conditions:

- **Core Stabilization:** Dependent on credit-financed consumption, military spending, dollar hegemony, and cheap imported raw materials.
- **Peripheral Stabilization:** Dependent on commodity export booms, state FX rationing, and import substitution.

The 1970s crisis of Fordism was a relational crisis where core and peripheral settlements collapsed simultaneously due to their mutual structural entanglement.

---

## Locked Formulation

Peripheral capacity utilization configurations are institutional settlements that internalize world-system structural constraints. Foreign exchange scarcity prevents the conversion of accounting surplus into imported machinery, reproducing Vidal's divided-management contradiction inside the peripheral firm as a non-reinvestment problem. Core and periphery operate on the same underlying logic of decentralized market anarchy and internal firm division, mediated through structurally differentiated and mutually entangled institutional configurations.
