# PROMPT: Articulating the Post-Fordist Decoupling Paradox (US $\gamma=4.22$)

## Context & Objective
The cointegration analysis reveals that the U.S. long-run elasticity between machinery ($K^{ME}$) and structures ($K^{NRC}$) shifted from balanced Fordist accumulation ($\gamma \approx 1$) to severe post-Fordist decoupling ($\gamma = 4.22$). 

Your task is to write the analytical exposition explaining this regime shift. You must resolve a core heterodox puzzle: **If the baseline heterogeneous Leontief model relies on distributive pressure (a rising wage share $\omega$) to induce mechanization, why did machinery accumulation violently decouple from plant expansion during the neoliberal era when labor was historically weak?** 

To resolve this, you must deploy the structural mechanics of **Marx-biased technical change** (Capital-Using, Labor-Saving) and the distributive logic of the **Marx-Okishio threshold**. The exposition must demonstrate that post-Fordist mechanization was not a defensive reaction to wage squeezes, but an offensive, labor-saving strategy that bypassed the tendency of the rate of profit to fall precisely *because* labor's structural power was broken.

## Execution Workflow
Develop the exposition by moving through the following three logical steps. Synthesize evidence and verdict in the same paragraph (WLM v4.0 voice).

### 1. Viable Technological Change & The Marx-Biased Frontier
- Disconnect the post-1973 mechanization wave from the classic wage-squeeze first-order condition. 
- Argue that the technological frontier shifted autonomously (e.g., the ICT revolution) into a regime of **Marx-biased technical change**: a structural pattern that is inherently Capital-Using and Labor-Saving (CU-LS). 
- Explain that the *expected profitability* of $K^{ME}$ investment became structurally viable independent of defensive wage-cost mitigation. Capitalists adopted $K^{ME}$ not to counter a rising $\omega$, but to unilaterally drive up labor productivity and reduce unit labor costs, initiating a trajectory where structural machinery capacity ($B^{ME}$) systematically stagnates or falls while labor productivity surges.

### 2. The Marx-Okishio Threshold & Rising Ex-Post Profitability
- Explain the distributive capture of this technical change using the logic of the **Marx-Okishio threshold**. 
- Establish that Marx-biased (CU-LS) technical change inherently carries a tendency to depress the general rate of profit due to rising capital intensity (falling $B^{ME}$). However, the actualization of this tendency is strictly conditional on the growth rate of the real wage.
- Because labor's structural bargaining power was broken (the absence of distributive pressure), the actual growth rate of the real wage was violently suppressed *below* the Marx-Okishio threshold. 
- Conclude that workers were unable to enforce a Goodwinian profit-squeeze or capture the productivity gains of the new $K^{ME}$. Consequently, the productivity-mechanization gap ($b^{ME}$) translated directly into rising ex-post profitability for capital. The neoliberal suppression of $\omega$ acted as the structural switch that transformed a potentially crisis-inducing Marx-biased technical change into an Okishio-style profitability boom.

### 3. The Unbalanced Nature of Accumulation (Why $\gamma = 4.22$)
- Ground the explanation in the time derivative of the cointegrating vector: $g_{K^{ME}} \approx \gamma \cdot g_{K^{NRC}}$. Explicitly state that $\gamma = 4.22$ proves machinery accumulation exploded at over 4 times the rate of structural plant expansion. 
- Clearly identify the dominant capital form: **$K^{ME}$ is the engine that is aggressively rising and accelerating, while $K^{NRC}$ is structurally stagnating.**
- Explain *why* this hyper-intensive accumulation occurred: Expanding the extensive margin ($K^{NRC}$) requires long-term spatial commitment and robust aggregate demand, both of which were choked off by neoliberal wage suppression. 
- Conversely, the intensive margin ($K^{ME}$) allowed firms to capture the Marx-biased technological frontier by stuffing tactical, labor-saving machinery into their existing, stagnating plant walls.
- Conclude that $\gamma = 4.22$ is the physical footprint of this contradiction: a profitability boom achieved through hyper-intensive mechanization ($K^{ME}$), housed within an atrophied structural envelope ($K^{NRC}$).
