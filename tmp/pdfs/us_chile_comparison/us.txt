                                                   Abstract
         This paper provides an integrated empirical audit of the long-run structural relationship between
     Nonresidential Structures (K NRC ) and Machinery and Equipment (K ME ) in the United States across
     a century of macroeconomic accumulation (1925–2021). Grounded in the political economy choice-
     of-technique framework, we frame capital equipment as the active, discretionary mechanization
     decision chosen by capitalists in response to distributional conflict, while structural plant footprint
     acts as the derived, accommodating physical envelope. We combine single-equation super-consistent
     estimators from the R cointReg package—Integrated Modified OLS (IM-OLS; Vogelsang & Wagner
     2014), Fully Modified OLS (FM-OLS; Phillips & Hansen 1990), and Dynamic OLS (DOLS; Stock
     & Watson 1993)—with Johansen VECM system models under drift and trend controls, empirical
     fluctuation processes (OLS-CUSUM), and Bai-Perron (1998, 2003) structural break tests.
         We report four central findings. First, across the Full Century (1925–2021), Johansen VECM
     Case 2 models with restricted drift controls identify a secular unit vector (γ̂ ≈ 0.994), proving
     that structural space scales proportionally with equipment over the secular long run to prevent
     chronic realization failures (τt = ktME − ktNRC ∼ I(0)). Second, in sub-period estimations, single-
     equation modified OLS estimators absorb differential growth velocities (dk̂/dt), revealing massive
     mechanization deepening during the Fordist Expansion (1947–1973; γ̂ ≈ 4.06–4.34) followed by
     regime collapse (γ̂ ≈ −0.60) during Neoliberal Restructuring (1974–2008). Third, VECM error-
     correction parameters (α) confirm bilateral adjustment during Fordism (αNR = −0.151, αME =
     −0.323), which completely dissolves under Neoliberalism (αNR ≈ 0.017, p > 0.30), marking the
     institutional decoupling of productive plant commitment from short-term financialized machinery
     turnover. Fourth, we include formal dummy variable controls mapped to non-parametric LOESS 2nd
     derivative zero-crossings (1933, 1968, and 2008), demonstrating that late-Fordist mechanization
     efficiency decelerated five years prior to the 1973 institutional collapse. An appendix provides a
     finite-sample mathematical proof showing why Johansen Case 3 VECMs mechanically converge to
     single-equation IM-OLS parameters under asymmetrical growth drifts.


1    Introduction: The Choice of Technique & Capital Heterogeneity
Capital is not a homogeneous blob of output. In classical and Marxian political economy, aggregate
accumulation fractures into two distinct physical assets whose stochastic trajectories record class
struggle and institutional crisis. Nonresidential structures (K NRC )—factories, power grids, warehouses—
embody the extensive margin of capacity formation: the physical envelope within which production
occurs. Machinery and equipment (K ME ) captures the intensive margin: the active choice of technique
through which capitalists respond to distributional pressure and competitive threat at the shop floor.
    The structural relationship normalized with Nonresidential Structures on the left-hand side:

a derived physical consequence. Structures do not generate value independently; their expansion is an

    When γ̂ ≈ 1.0, structures growth tracks machinery accumulation proportionally—a Harrodian
balanced growth corridor in which plant expansion and technique choice move in tandem. When
γ̂ > 1.0 (or γ̂ ≫ 1.0 under static single-equation estimators), machinery density accelerates relative to
physical plant space. When γ̂ turns negative or cointegration fails (r = 0), the two capital forms have
decoupled so sharply that no stable long-run equilibrium binds them, signaling institutional regime
breakdown.
    This paper provides a complete empirical audit of this structural relationship across the United
States macro-eras (1925–2021), rigorously separating single-equation cointegrating elasticities (γ) from
system error-correction adjustment speeds (α) to evaluate institutional friction and drift leakage.

    Figure 5 and Table 2 reveal the institutional transmission mechanism:
    • Fordist Bilateral Planning: During the Golden Age (1947–1973), both adjustment terms
      are negative and statistically significant (αNR = −0.151, αME = −0.323). Machinery adjusted
      more than twice as fast as structural space (| − 0.323| > | − 0.151|). Heavy plant construction
      exhibited structural inertia (slow adjustment of 15% per year), while machinery turnover provided
      operational flexibility (32% adjustment per year) to restore long-run balance.
    • Neoliberal Institutional Decoupling: Under Neoliberal Restructuring (1974–2008), αNR →
      0.017 (p = 0.36), losing statistical significance. Plant footprint completely lost its error-correcting
      role. Firms stopped adjusting long-term structural plant commitments to capacity disequilibrium,
      focusing instead on short-term ICT equipment turnover and domestic plant deindustrialization.

Dummy Variable Strategy
To control for these structural shifts without fracturing the cointegrating rank, we incorporate: 1.
**Pre-War Step Control (D1925−1946 )**: Absorbs Great Depression volatility and WWII defense plant
construction, allowing us to stretch the Fordist window back to 1925 (N = 49) while preserving
post-war Golden Age elasticity. 2. **Late-Fordist Squeeze Control (Dsqueeze : 1968–1973)**: Mapped
directly to the 1968 LOESS zero-crossing, controlling for profit-squeeze deceleration prior to the 1973
Bretton Woods rupture. 3. **Post-GFC Control (DGFC : 2009–2021)**: Captures ZLB monetary
stagnation and share-buyback financialization.

6     Admissibility & Capacity Realization (τt ∼ I(0))
For downstream capacity utilization modeling, the integration order of the capital composition state
variable τt = ktME − ktNRC acts as a binding admissibility gate.
    If k ME and k NRC fail to cointegrate, τt inherits non-stationary I(1) drift, creating chronic realization
crises where physical plant space cannot accommodate machinery output. Over the Full Century,
Johansen Case 2 confirms γ̂ = 0.994, proving that τt is a stationary I(0) state variable. This validates
τt as an admissible, mathematically bounded proxy for capacity utilization (µt ) in macro-modelling.
