                A WBOP-Based Admissibility and Event Audit, 1920–2024

                                               Abstract

        This report replaces the former Chile capital-composition report with a pre-estimation audit.
    It adopts the investigative sequence of the United States capital-composition report—visual
    history, integration orders, cointegration choice, stability, and bounded interpretation—without
    importing United States estimates or imposing its model on Chile. The historical external-sector
    evidence comes exclusively from the World Historical Balance of Payments database (WBOP).
    The WBOP workbook supplies complete annual Chile series for trade, income, transfers, the
    current account, and foreign wealth over 1920–2024, and its accounting identities close to
    machine precision. It does not identify machinery imports, gross capital inflows, reserve use,
    debt service, or essential imports jointly. The A04 conversion ratio ϕt is therefore latent and is
    not reconstructed.
        The admissibility battery produces the decisive result. Sixteen external candidates receive an
    I(1) verdict and two foreign-wealth candidates receive an I(0) verdict, but ktME , ktNR , and κt =
    ktME − ktNR remain unresolved because their first differences do not secure a stationary majority
    across ADF, PP, KPSS, ERS–GLS, and break-allowing tests. The active gate consequently
    blocks Johansen, IM–OLS, FM–OLS, DOLS, and degree-2 CPR interaction estimates. This is
    not an absence of evidence: it overturns the former report’s unsupported elasticity and prevents
    a non-admissible regression from becoming a historical conclusion. Annual WBOP financing-
    compression events in 1937, 1976, 1999, and 2023 coincide with a larger contemporaneous
    reduction in machinery accumulation than in structures, but the four-episode bootstrap interval
    includes zero. The evidence establishes external-pressure timing and a machinery-sensitive
    pattern; it does not yet identify a stable long-run external-constraint coefficient.

1    Empirical Problem and the A04 Measurement Boundary

Chile’s two-capital history presents a concrete identification problem. Machinery and equipment KtME
is the import-dependent intensive margin, while nonresidential structures KtNR is the predominantly
domestic extensive margin. That distinction is a maintained analytical restriction. The available
stocks do not reveal the imported fraction of each investment flow, and the WBOP manufactured-
goods import series is broader than capital goods. The report therefore tests the observable
implication—whether external pressure is associated with a relative interruption of machinery
accumulation—without relabelling manufactured imports as machinery imports.

A04 defines foreign-exchange availability as exports plus net capital inflows and reserve use, net
of debt service and essential imports, and compares that envelope with consistently measured
machinery-import requirements. WBOP observes the long historical current account and foreign-
wealth positions, but it does not provide every term required for that conversion ratio in one
consistent Chile series. Hence Ftavail , Ftreq , ν, and ϕt remain analytical objects. No numerical value
is assigned to them. The former report’s exports-only envelope and 0.15 import-intensity calibration
are removed because they converted missing measurement into artificial abundance.

The same boundary applies to capacity utilization. No cumulative GDP peak is used to construct
µt . Capacity utilization requires an independently reconstructed productive-capacity level and its
active anchoring protocol; this capital-composition audit neither supplies nor substitutes for that
reconstruction.

The United States report contributes only an audit architecture: inspect the raw histories, establish
integration orders, adjudicate the estimator, test breaks and system stability, then issue a verdict.
Chile’s data decide which stages survive. No United States coefficient, regime, or comparison enters
this report.

Four WBOP identities were checked year by year: goods exports less goods imports equals the goods
balance; manufactured exports less manufactured imports equals the manufactured balance; goods
and services plus net foreign income plus transfers equals the current account; and gross foreign
assets less gross foreign liabilities equals net foreign wealth. Their maximum absolute residuals
range from 5.1 × 10−16 to 7.0 × 10−15 . The workbook therefore closes internally for Chile, and the
extracted signs and sheet assignments are verified.

Manufactured-goods imports, the current account, net foreign income, and gross foreign liabilities
carry distinct historical information. Their divergence is economically meaningful: trade dependence,
income drainage, net financing need, and accumulated foreign obligations are not interchangeable
measures of a single latent scarcity index. The model therefore introduces one candidate at a time
and never averages them into an undocumented composite.

LOESS derivatives are retained only as visual inflection markers. Formal Bai–Perron results locate
multiple breaks in each capital series: 1934, 1949, 1964, 1989, and 2009 for ktME ; 1934, 1949, 1971,
1988, and 2009 for ktNR ; and 1945, 1962, 1994, and 2009 for κt . SupF and CUSUM tests reject
stability for every series. The evidence rejects a full-sample invariant slope before the external
candidates even enter.



4    Integration and Break Audit

Every capital and WBOP candidate is tested in levels and first differences with ADF, Phillips–Perron,
KPSS, ERS–GLS, and Zivot–Andrews procedures under constant and trend specifications. Annual
autoregressive corrections are capped at four lags, with ADF selecting within that bound by AIC. A

Sixteen external candidates pass as I(1), and two pass as I(0), but the capital variables fail the
first-difference gate. This pattern is consistent with very smooth stock accumulation, multiple
level and slope breaks, or higher-order persistence; the present battery does not distinguish those
possibilities decisively. The verdict is therefore held, not forced I(1). Because the dependent and
benchmark capital variables fail first, no external candidate can rescue the cointegration design.



5    Capital-Composition Benchmark

The intended Layer-2 relation is

                                            ktNR = α + βME ktME + εt ,

where βME belongs to the cointegrating vector. The bivariate benchmark is specification CL-L2-BIV-001.
Its data, estimator menu, sample, and release hash are recorded in the model ledger before estimation.

The benchmark is blocked because neither capital series has an admissible I(1) verdict. Estimating
a Johansen rank or an IM–OLS slope under these conditions would make cointegration inference
depend on an integration order the audit has rejected. The former report’s elasticity near 0.88 is
therefore withdrawn; it has no valid successor in this release.

This blockage narrows the next empirical task. The capital stocks require an explicit I(2)-risk and
multiple-break investigation, followed by a decision between an I(2) system, break-adjusted I(1)
representation, or accumulation-rate model. That extension must pass a new gate before the level
relation can be reopened.

6       WBOP Candidate Adjudication

income, transfer, and gross-asset candidates that pass as I(1) may eventually enter one-at-a-time

restricted to exogenous short-run, impulse, or deterministic roles. No stock series is relabelled as a

The WBOP current account can measure an annual external-financing requirement through −CA/Y ,
but it cannot supply a Forbes–Warnock gross-inflow sudden stop. This report therefore names the
observed object “annual WBOP financing compression” and does not claim that it is a gross-flow
event. That distinction prevents a current-account reversal from being misreported as a directly
observed capital-flow collapse.



7       VECM and Super-Consistent Results

The S30 script contains the complete conditional estimator implementation. When both capital
variables pass as I(1), it searches Johansen deterministic cases and lag lengths, saves ranks and
normalized vectors, estimates adjustment coefficients, evaluates serial correlation and normality,
and checks companion-root stability. The single-equation menu assigns IM–OLS the baseline role,
FM–OLS the linear diagnostic role, and DOLS the fragility role.

None of those branches executes in the present release. Every S30 result file is saved, but the
coefficient, rank-vector, adjustment, and diagnostic tables are empty by construction; the specification
ledger records “blocked” and the failed prerequisite for every proposed model. This is the econometric

9    Annual External Pressure and Growth Evidence

The primary annual event rule records years in which the WBOP external-financing proxy −CA/Y
falls by at least five percentage points. Growth, investment, and capital accumulation do not enter
the event definition. The rule identifies 1937, 1976, 1999, and 2023. Because WBOP does not observe
historical gross inflows at the required frequency and decomposition, these are financing-compression
episodes rather than Forbes–Warnock sudden stops.

At the event year, mean machinery accumulation falls to 1.30 percent while structures accumulate
at 2.43 percent, so ∆κt falls by 1.13 percentage points. One year later, machinery remains below
structures by 0.60 percentage points. The direction matches the maintained import-dependence
restriction, but the episode bootstrap for the mean post-event machinery-minus-structures difference
spans [−2.36, 0.55] percentage points. The interval includes zero; timing supports a machinery-
sensitive pattern, but the four events do not establish a stable differential response.

The GDP evidence is weaker. Only three events fall inside the 1920–2010 real-GDP coverage, and
mean growth is positive in the event and following year. Event overlap therefore does not support a
claim that every financing compression produces an immediate output contraction. Growth remains
an outcome, and the report rejects causal language.

Threshold sensitivity confirms that episode selection matters: a three-point rule yields 14 events,
four points yields eight, five points yields four, and six points yields two. Leave-one-episode-out,
pre/post-1974, and placebo ledgers are retained. The event evidence is descriptive and bounded
because the result changes with the threshold and the historical event count is small.

The historical WBOP evidence changes the Chile research design in three ways. First, it replaces
a fabricated scalar scarcity ratio with observed and separately interpretable balance-of-payments
candidates. Second, it dates annual financing-compression episodes independently of growth and
finds a machinery-sensitive response whose uncertainty remains wide. Third, it blocks the long-run

capital-composition model because the capital stocks do not satisfy the active I(1) gate. These are
affirmative adjudications, not unfinished regressions.

The bounded verdict is therefore precise. Chile’s external accounts contain long-run pressure
variables capable of disciplining A04, and the event evidence is consistent with a relative machinery
interruption during financing compression. The current release does not identify ϕt , a permanent
external regime, a cointegrating vector, or a causal external-constraint coefficient. The next
admissible step is an I(2)-risk and multiple-break capital-stock audit; only its outcome can reopen
Johansen, IM–OLS, and CPR estimation.

B     Research and Integrity Checkpoints

The research checkpoint verified the WBOP coverage, workbook, conceptual identities, and official
replication claim against https://wbop.world/ and Nievas and Piketty’s 2025 working paper. The
data checkpoint froze the workbook and closed the four accounting identities. The econometric
checkpoint kept all conflicting capital classifications held and prevented interaction promotion. The
failure-mode checkpoint searched the new pipeline and report for the former calibrated objects, legacy
notation, Markdown bold inside LaTeX, prohibited float specifications, and FWL residualization.
The adversarial checkpoint asked whether the event rule observes gross inflows, whether manufactured
imports directly measure machinery, and whether timing identifies causality; the answer is no in all
three cases, and the prose states each boundary.
