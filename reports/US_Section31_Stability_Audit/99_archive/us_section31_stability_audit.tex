\section*{Executive Summary}
The Section 3.1 IM-OLS estimation matrix reported overwhelming evidence \emph{against} cointegration. This audit finds that verdict was substantially an artifact of test mis-specification. The residual Engle--Granger test was implemented as an \emph{unaugmented} Dickey--Fuller regression (zero lagged differences) while being compared against MacKinnon critical values tabulated for the \emph{augmented} test. The IM-OLS residuals carry AR(2)--AR(4) structure with first-order autocorrelation between 0.81 and 0.96; lag augmentation is precisely the device that whitens such errors, and without it the test is severely biased toward non-rejection.

Correcting the augmentation reverses the finding: rejections of the null of no cointegration rise from \textbf{2 of 18} models to \textbf{16 of 18} at the 5\% level (16 of 18 under BIC; 18 of 18 at the 10\% level). AIC and BIC select the same lag order in 17 of 18 models, and even minimal $p=1$ augmentation flips the verdict, so the reversal is not an artifact of lag selection.

A genuine secondary finding survives the correction. The underlying relationship \emph{is} structurally unstable: the CUSUM family rejects parameter stability in 18 of 18 models, with break dates at 1974, 1948 and 1945, and the Gregory--Hansen regime-shift scan locates a break in 1976--77 in 15 of 18 models. That instability is real, but it is absorbed by the Bai--Perron step dummies already present in the specification --- it was never the reason the cointegration tests failed. Note that the Gregory--Hansen \emph{statistic} rejects its own null of no cointegration in only 1 of 18 models at 5\%: a single regime shift is not sufficient where the Bai--Perron audit identifies three to five level shifts.

\section{What the Failure Was Not: Regressor Admissibility}
The leading rival explanation --- that the interaction regressors inherit $I(2)$ behaviour and are inadmissible in an $I(1)$ cointegration framework --- is excluded. The S33 integration-order battery had already tested the interaction terms directly and returned $I(1)$ throughout. This audit closed two gaps that battery left open, testing the series \emph{exactly as US\_S50 constructs them}: the \textbf{lagged} interaction terms (S33 tested contemporaneous ones), and \texttt{omega\_ST\_Tier2\_GVA\_adj} standalone, which had never been tested despite being the wage share the project constitution mandates.

\subsection{Reconciling Hansen with Shin}
Shin's $C$ statistic is Hansen's $L_c$ restricted to intercept-only parameter variation. The identity $L_c^{\text{intercept}} = T \cdot C_{\text{Shin}}$ was verified exactly across all 18 models (maximum relative error $3\times10^{-16}$) and is enforced as a blocking pre-condition in the audit script. The v2 report's 18/18 Shin rejection was therefore always a statement about \emph{intercept} instability, not about cointegration failure in general. Its apparent conflict with the non-rejecting $L_c$ reported above is a long-run-variance normalisation difference: Shin uses \texttt{cointReg}'s conditional $\omega_{u\cdot v}$, Hansen the score-process Bartlett LRV.

\subsection{Where the instability sits}
The per-parameter $L_c$ decomposition locates the instability in the \textbf{step-dummy coefficients} rather than the structural parameters. Representative values (model M1.1): intercept 4.4, $\hat\theta_0$ 4.6, $\hat\theta_1$ 2.7, against $D_{1965}$ 20.4, $D_{1985}$ 20.1, $D_{2003}$ 17.3. The capital elasticity is comparatively stable; what moves is the level. These magnitudes are \emph{descriptive only} --- see Section~6.

\FloatBarrier
\section{What This Audit Does Not Claim}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The hand-rolled Hansen subset statistic is not used for inference.} $L_c^{\text{core}}$ inverts the submatrix $V_{\text{core,core}}$, ignoring covariance with the nuisance step-dummy scores. It failed a self-normalisation check --- independent i.i.d. and AR-sieve nulls produced materially different distributions --- whereas the joint $L_c$ agreed across both to within 1\%. Inferential instability verdicts therefore come from \texttt{strucchange}'s published asymptotics.
\item \textbf{A null result was not reinterpreted as a finding.} The first bootstrap run returned Hansen $p \approx 0.96$ (``parameters are stable''), contradicting every other diagnostic. That was treated as an implementation problem to investigate rather than a result; investigating it is what surfaced the ADF defect.
\item \textbf{A defect in this audit's own code was found and fixed mid-run.} The Gregory--Hansen inner ADF initially replicated the same unaugmented-DF bug being audited, inherited from the repository's existing convention. The first production run was discarded.
\item \textbf{Break dates are reported as they came out}, including the disagreement between CUSUM (1974), Gregory--Hansen (1977), and the Bai--Perron dates already in the estimation matrix (1944, 1965--68, 1981--82, 1996--99, 2010). No date was selected to fit a narrative.
\item \textbf{Shin LM critical values remain unverified}, carried over from the preceding significance-star audit.
\end{enumerate}
