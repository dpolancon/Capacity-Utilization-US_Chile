## 1. Sectoral Boundary Architecture & Theoretical Setup

National accounting consistency requires strict clarity regarding sectoral boundaries:
- **Observed Aggregate Output ($Y_t$)**: Always corresponds to *Total Corporate Gross Value Added* ($Y_{\text{CORP}}$) or Aggregate Real Output ($Y_t$) in NIPA accounts, capturing overall demand realization across the corporate sector.
- **Productive Capital Stock ($K^{\text{cap}}$) & Productive Capacity ($Y^p_t$)**: Derived strictly from *Non-Financial Corporate* (NFC) fixed assets ($K_{\text{NFC}}^{\text{ME}} + K_{\text{NFC}}^{\text{NRC}}$). Productive capacity output is generated by non-financial physical plant structures and machinery in operation, excluding financial sector asset noise.

Capacity utilization ($\mu_t = Y_t / Y^p_t$) is constructed as observed corporate output relative to estimated non-financial productive capacity. While extensive plant structures ($K^{\text{NRC}}$) and intensive machinery ($K^{\text{ME}}$) drive physical output capacity, the *choice of technique* is governed by distributional conflict. Sunk investment horizons mean that nonresidential structures evolve independently of short-run profit squeezes ($\frac{\partial K^{\text{NRC}}}{\partial \omega} = 0$), whereas tactical machinery investment responds directly to lagged wage shares ($\omega_{t-1}$).

## 3. Reconstructing Capacity Utilization ($\mu_t$)

Following vault protocol D03, I anchored the level scale by declaring $t^*_{\text{US}} = 1973$ as the full-capacity reference pinch year ($\mu_{\text{US}, 1973} = 1.00$). The reconstructed capacity utilization series ($\hat{\mu}_t$) captures key historical peaks and troughs:
- WWII peak: $\hat{\mu}_{1944} \approx 1.15$
- Fordist Golden Age stability: $\hat{\mu}_t \in [0.90, 1.00]$ (1947–1973)
- Neoliberal structural decline & Great Recession trough: $\hat{\mu}_{2009} \approx 0.70$

## 4. The Long-Run Overaccumulation Regime ($\theta_t < 1$)

The total transformation elasticity measuring how aggregate capital accumulation expands productive capacity is defined by:
$$\theta_t = \frac{\partial y_t^p}{\partial k_t^{\text{cap}}} = \hat{\beta}_{\text{Kcap}} + \hat{\theta}_1 \omega_{t-1}$$
Across all specifications, $\theta_t$ remains bounded below 1.00 throughout the entire 1929–2024 sample period ($\theta_t \in [0.65, 0.95]$), establishing that **the U.S. capitalist economy has operated persistently within a structural Overaccumulation Regime for nearly a century.**
